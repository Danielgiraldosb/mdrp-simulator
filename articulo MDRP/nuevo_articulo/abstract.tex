\begin{abstract}
The Meal Delivery Routing Problem (MDRP) requires assigning couriers
to orders and computing delivery routes under stochastic demand,
tight customer time windows, and heterogeneous courier fleets.
We propose a Biased Random-Key Genetic Algorithm with Rolling Horizon
(BRKGA-RH) for the MDRP and evaluate two fitness variants ---
BRKGA-Coverage (maximising orders delivered) and BRKGA-Travel
(minimising average travel time) --- against two greedy baselines:
a Business Model heuristic and a Business Model Lookahead that
defers low-volume stores via demand forecasting.
All four methods share the same rolling-horizon architecture,
re-solving at each 2-minute window.
Experiments use 24 real-world instances from a Latin American
food-delivery platform (538--59{,}167 orders).
On small and medium instances, both BRKGA variants achieve an average
coverage rate of 59.2\%, outperforming the Business Model Lookahead
(51.4\%) and the Business Model (44.1\%) by 8 and 15 percentage
points, respectively.
On large instances the ranking reverses: the Business Model Lookahead
reaches 51.1\% versus 47.2\% for BRKGA-Coverage, as the bounded
generation budget limits metaheuristic search on high-volume days.
Both BRKGA variants are computationally faster than the Business
Model Lookahead (138\,s and 105\,s versus 589\,s on average).
BRKGA-Travel delivers equivalent coverage while running 24\% faster
than BRKGA-Coverage, making it the preferred choice for
small-to-medium deployments; the Business Model Lookahead is
recommended for large instances.
A hybrid strategy switching between methods based on daily order
volume captures the best of both approaches.

\keywords{Meal Delivery Routing Problem \and Biased Random-Key
Genetic Algorithm \and Rolling Horizon \and Dynamic Vehicle Routing
\and Food Delivery Platforms}
\end{abstract}
