# Referee-style review of `main.tex` (EJOR, Innovative Applications of O.R.)

Date: 2026-10-31. Manuscript: commit d283368 (47 pp.; body and references to p. 35; 16 `\todo` marks, 15 of them the author issues #1–#10). Three independent readings (model and method; computational study and statistics; positioning and presentation) were cross-checked and every item below marked **[verified]** was confirmed by me against the .tex, the compiled PDF, the analysis code or the instance files. Items marked **[plausible]** are the readers' judgement calls that I agree with but did not reproduce numerically. Line numbers refer to `docs/paper-cmdp/paper/main.tex` at that commit.

## Recommendation: major revision

The problem is relevant and the paper's real contribution is sound and valuable: an end-of-day fairness statistic over a population the dispatcher cannot shrink, the demonstration that the usual active-courier Gini tells the opposite story, the diagnosis of why history-aware order-centric policies fail (content of the history and candidate set), and a remedy that halves the gap at no coverage cost, all on real operations with field-calibrated behaviour and sound experimental hygiene. A senior EJOR referee would nevertheless return it, for five reasons that recur across the three readings:

1. **The framing promises what the results do not deliver.** Title, abstract and contributions speak of a "guarantee" that no proposed policy enforces or meets at the band the paper itself calls low inequality; the "first to" claims collide with the paper's own Table 1.
2. **The formulation and the implemented policies disagree on the object at the heart of the paper.** The state defines active time as connected time, the first-generation policies read in-motion time, which is not in the state as written, and the second-generation "exposure" reading is what the formulation already says. The deficit formula saturates at 1 for every idle courier, so the mechanism the text attributes to the second generation ("the deficit grows with every hour it waits") is not what the formula does.
3. **Numbers in the abstract, introduction, findings and conclusion do not match the tables** (coverage spread, Pay-BRKGA and coverage, gap-closed percentages computed by an undefined statistic, compliance claims contradicted by the compliance table, a column that changes definition between tables, a selection rule stated but not applied).
4. **Two of the 24 instances share the same orders file.** Instances 1 and 3 (Bucaramanga) have byte-identical `orders.csv` and `stores.csv` and differ only in the courier file. The paper calls them "different days with identical counts". Until this is resolved, N is 23 order-days and every standard error, bootstrap interval and compliance percentage is affected.
5. **Presentation:** the two central figures are illegible, the bold convention marks the worst value of the minimum-wage deficit as best, notation is overloaded (π, w, T, b, K, λ, e), the reference list prints BibTeX note fields ("Source: thesis.", "TODO ..."), and the abstract has 403 words.

None of this requires new modelling; most of it requires no new runs. The correction plan at the end separates what can be fixed in the text and tables, what needs runs, and what needs an author decision.

## A. Claims versus results

**A1. "Guarantee" and "first" claims. [verified]** The title says "an end-of-day earnings guarantee"; l.141 claims "the first MDRP formulation in which fairness is guaranteed at the end of the day rather than per window"; l.211 "the first to control an end-of-day inequality constraint on real meal-delivery operations". Yet l.591: no first-generation configuration meets α ≤ 0.40 on any day; the second generation meets α = 0.45 on 61–67 % of the (instance, seed) pairs and none at 0.30; l.550: the α of MIP-Fair "has no measurable effect ... including α = 1.00, which switches the constraint off". Nothing in the three policies enforces Eq. (fair_const): no dual price, no terminal feasibility check, no adaptation across the day. Table 1 lists FairFoody (gupta2022fairfoody) with guarantee horizon "day", which contradicts the first "first". *Fix:* reserve "guarantee" for the definition of the statistic and the compliance reading; claim the conjunction explicitly ("the first to combine an end-of-day Gini constraint over a dispatcher-proof population, an equity history in the state and price-endogenous acceptance"); make l.205 ("fairness is enforced per window or per instance, or through a reward term") consistent with the "day" rows of Table 1; consider a title without "guarantee" (author decision). Add one sentence in §3.2 saying that Eq. (fair_const) constrains a terminal non-linear functional, so CMDP solution theory (Altman) does not apply directly, which is why cost function approximations are used.

**A2. Three readings of "the guarantee". [verified]** Eq. (fair_const) is an expectation conditional on S_0; compliance (l.495) is a frequency over (instance, seed) pairs, i.e. a chance constraint P(F_G ≤ α) ≥ 1 − β with β unreported; MIP-Fair enforces a deterministic per-round surrogate on projected values. The two first readings already disagree on the benchmark: Greedy meets α = 0.30 in expectation (mean 0.301) but on 50 % of the pairs. *Fix:* state the chance-constrained form as the operational one, name β ("met on most days" is β ≈ 0.4), and say in §4 that the three policies are heuristics for either form.

**A3. Introduction insights and abstract numbers that do not match the tables. [verified against Table 3 (tab:benchmark)]**
- l.134 "coverage differs by less than one point between policies"; l.135 "at most 1.2 between policies"; l.664 "the whole spread of coverage between policies within the manual regime (3.2 points)". Table 3 manual: 0.738 (ILP) to 0.770 (BRKGA) = 3.2 points; Greedy against the best order-centric policy = 1.2 points. One definition everywhere.
- l.136 "Pay-BRKGA keeps coverage with 12 % (manual) to 20 % (auto-accept) less total pay" versus Table 3 manual 0.753 against 0.758 (Greedy) and 0.770 (BRKGA), and l.664 "in the manual regime the pay lever is paid for in coverage". Also l.546 "every other policy except ILP delivers at least as much with less lateness" is false for Pay-BRKGA.
- l.137 "98–100 % remain below [the minimum wage]" versus F_D = 0.973 (Greedy, auto-accept): write 97–100 %.
- Abstract and conclusion "between 0.52 and 0.57" versus Table 3 ILP auto-accept 0.501: say "manual regime" or write 0.50–0.57.
- l.679 "at a cost of 1.1–1.2 points of coverage" under both regimes: under auto-accept every configuration covers 0.786, so the coverage cost is zero there.
- l.133 "only the policies that read H_t as exposure ... halve that gap": the policy that closes the whole gap is Greedy, which reads ord_c(t) (l.476), so "only" misleads.

**A4. The gap-closed percentages are computed by an undefined statistic. [verified in `simulator/analyze_gen2.py` l.44 and `analyze_supp.py` l.95]** Abstract, l.552, l.677 and the conclusion give 45–57 % (k_d = 25) and 59–81 % (k_d = 50). From the table means: BRKGA-Fair k_d = 25 closes (0.534 − 0.437)/(0.534 − 0.301) = 42 % manual and 50 % auto-accept; k_d = 50 closes 56 % and 77 %; MIP-Fair 45 % and 56 %. The code averages the per-(instance, seed) ratios, which is a different estimand and is never stated. *Fix:* define the statistic once in §5.4, say which Greedy (five-seed) is the reference, and add a "gap closed [95 % CI]" column to Table 4 and to the k_d = 50 rows of Table C.11, or switch to the ratio of means everywhere.

**A5. Abstract. [verified: 403 words]** House style is 180–230 words with two key figures. The last two sentences hold nine numbers; bold policy names are unusual in an EJOR abstract; "select α by three criteria" states a method without its result.

## B. Model and method

**B1. The time base of the equity history is inconsistent, and it is the crux of the paper. [verified: l.248, l.286, l.305, l.398, l.408, l.550]** Eq. (history) defines act_c(t) as "active (connected) time" and the transition adds "2 minutes for every connected courier, accepted or not", so act_c is connected time; l.305 then introduces a second symbol conn_c(T) for the same quantity; l.398 defines w_c(t) = earn_c(t)/act_c(t) and says the hourly basis is "chosen for coherence with Eq. (gini)"; but l.408 and l.550 say the first-generation policies read "earnings per active hour, minutes in motion or serving, which is a productivity rate", a quantity that is not in S_t as defined. As written, the formulation already contains the exposure basis and the "diagnosis" of §6 reads as the correction of a mismatch between model and code. The word "active" carries three meanings (connected time; in-motion time; couriers with ≥ 1 delivery). *Fix:* h_c(t) = (earn_c, ord_c, conn_c, busy_c); define w_c(t) = earn_c/conn_c once in §3; write Eqs. (brkgafair) and (mipfair_w) with conn_c; present the in-motion basis as the ablation it is; use "active" for one meaning only.

**B2. The deficit of Eq. (brkgafair) saturates at 1 for every idle courier. [verified in `simulator/brkga_policy.py` l.113: "among equal deficits (every idle zero-earner has 1) the nearest"]** deficit_c = max(0, w̄_t − w_c(t))/w̄_t equals 1 whenever earn_c = 0, after one minute or six hours. Hence "the k_d free couriers with the largest deficit, the nearest among ties" is, whenever ≥ k_d couriers are idle, "the k_d nearest idle couriers", and the exposure basis changes nothing for idle couriers (consistent with the paper's own finding that the basis alone is almost inert). The explanation at l.552 and l.677 ("the deficit of an idle courier grows with every hour it waits") is not what the formula does. *Fix (text):* rewrite the mechanism as "the prefilter puts the nearest idle couriers in front of the solver; exposure matters only for couriers with positive earnings". *Fix (method, author decision):* a deficit on an absolute scale (w̄_t · conn_c − earn_c, in COP) or a tie-break by connected time, with the k_d variants rerun.

**B3. Decision and pay objects. [verified: Eq. (decision), l.286, l.301, l.405, l.427]** Eq. (decision) defines x_t as the set of all feasible triples, not a selection (should be x_t ⊆ ...). pay(c, s, π_{c,s}) is never related to π_{c,s} (equal when accepted?) and later loses its arguments (pay(s), Σ pay). Earnings are credited at acceptance but orders "when the route is completed" (two update times for one vector), while the MIP projection credits pay and τ_s at dispatch.

**B4. "Special cases" is not a containment. [plausible]** With α = 1 the process still has Bernoulli acceptance and a pay decision; Reyes et al. is recovered only with p = 1 and m_c = 1; Wen et al. and Liao et al. are multi-objective models over days or an instance; WORK4FOOD controls fleet size and pays idle time. What is true is that the constraint slot admits F_O or F_D. Phrase it so.

**B5. Eligibility at round t, connection information and the prefilters are not reimplementable. [verified: l.221 vs l.279; "dispatching radius" l.552 undefined; k = 8 vs 25 l.499]** Eligibility is an end-of-day property (conn_c(T) ≥ 1 h); the text does not say how a policy decides at round t who is eligible. l.221 says connection times are "known in advance", l.279 says log-ins and log-outs are revealed exogenously. The k_d set: per route or global; subject to feasibility or not; "nearest" by distance or travel time; what the radius is. Second-generation MIP-Fair has 8 + k_d candidates and BRKGA-Fair 25 + k_d, so the k_d effect is not like for like. *Fix:* pseudocode for the prefilter; define C^e_t; state the asymmetry in Table 4.

**B6. Pay-BRKGA cannot be reimplemented from the text. [verified: l.342 vs l.408 and Table 4 row "Pay-BRKGA (exposure, k_d = 8)"; l.266 vs l.423; l.298 vs l.431]** l.342 says it drops the deficit bias; l.408, l.552 and Table 4 say it reads a deficit with the exposure basis. F_D^proj is essentially 1 for every chromosome (w* lies above the 99th percentile of every distribution, l.626), so λ_def is inert and the policy is in practice "minimise pay subject to expected coverage": say so. The multiplier applies to the whole tariff (l.423) but l.266 says "around the tariff" for the affine form; λ of Eq. (cost_plus) is absolute, λ_pay relative. The pay gene is per courier, so the route-dependent mechanism of l.626 needs explanation.

**B7. What the "block-exact linearisation" makes exact is narrower than claimed; the scalability sentence is contradicted by the run times. [verified: l.141 vs l.499, l.552, l.771, l.1049]** The algebra (Σ_{i<j}|w_i − w_j| ≤ α n Σ w, tight epigraph d_ij, three blocks) is correct, and the toy verification is good practice; but what is exact is the linearisation of a deterministic surrogate that (a) assumes acceptance, (b) is over C_t, not C^e, (c) advances the denominator of dispatched couriers by τ_s and nobody else's, (d) has v ≈ n (F_G − α), not a dimensionless Gini excess, and α → 0 minimises the mean absolute difference, not the Gini. The introduction's "solvable in a fraction of a second with thousands of connected couriers" is contradicted by 4.5 s per round in Bogotá with k = 8, by cutting k to 8 for budget, by rounds hitting the 10-s limit and by 11–110 minutes per day in the second generation. *Fix:* list (a)–(d) in §4, give variable counts as functions of |C_t^free|, n_fixed and |K_t|, replace the sentence by measured numbers.

**B8. The verification claim is broader than what §4 reports. [plausible]** For BRKGA-Fair only the coverage decoder is checked against enumeration; Pay-BRKGA checks are directional; the toy instance should have busy couriers for the free–fixed block to be exercised. Add an all-pairs versus block comparison on completed rounds of a real instance (Pereira).

**B9. ILP and MIP-Fair do not share candidate pairs, which confounds the comparison meant to isolate the Gini constraint. [verified: l.499 k = 25 for all policies except MIP-Fair k = 8; Table 3 manual ILP 0.738/0.521 vs MIP-Fair 0.764/0.544; l.550 MIP-Fair α = 1.00 gives 0.544–0.545, not the ILP values]** l.419 "α = 1 recovers the coverage ILP" and l.478 "comparing the two isolates the value of the Gini constraint" do not hold in the implemented system. A fairness-constrained policy covering 2.6 points more than the pure coverage ILP with three times fewer candidates under manual acceptance also needs an explanation. *Fix:* run the ILP with k = 8 (or report MIP-Fair α = 1 as the ILP), explain the manual-regime gap, correct l.128/l.340/l.473.

**B10. The "corporate" Greedy embeds a courier-centric fairness rule. [verified: l.203 vs l.476]** §2 describes Greedy as "each takes its nearest feasible route"; §5 adds "visited in increasing order of the orders they have delivered so far". Least-served-first is a fairness priority rule and reads H_t; no field evidence is cited that the platform orders couriers this way. The central result (Greedy spreads work), Criterion A ("status quo") and the answer to RQ1 depend on it. *Fix:* cite the evidence or add a courier-centric benchmark with arrival/random order so that "courier-centric" and "least-served-first" are separated; describe Greedy identically in §2 and §5.

**B11. The second generation is introduced inside the results. [verified: l.408, Alg. 1 l.348 parenthesis, l.552 (600-word paragraph)]** A reader finishing §4 does not know what the recommended policy is; "parents" is confusing in a paper whose core heuristic is a genetic algorithm. *Fix:* define exposure basis and deficit-aware prefilter in §4 with k_d as a parameter beside w, α, λ_pay; present the activity basis and k_d = 0 as ablations; replace "first/second generation" and "parents" by "activity basis / exposure basis" and "k_d = 0 / 25"; list k_d in the factors table.

## C. Experiments and statistics

**C1. The F_G^act column changes definition between tables. [verified in `tables_paper.py` l.111/161 (uses `F_Gini`, the simulator's active-courier statistic) and `analyze_supp.py` l.104 (uses `gini_elig_active_hourly`)]** Table 3/4 report, e.g., MIP-Fair F_G^act = 0.460 and BRKGA-Fair 0.500; Table C.11 reports 0.363–0.380 for configurations within 0.001 of those bases, and l.1049 "the active-courier Gini from 0.365 to 0.330" inherits the mismatch. *Fix:* one definition and one column name everywhere; state the population in every caption.

**C2. The "best configuration per regime" rule is stated but not applied, and selection is on the evaluation data. [verified: l.510 rule; Table 3 BRKGA-Fair w = 20 (0.537) while w = 40 gives 0.534 in Table 4 and in `main_couriers_all.txt`]** By the stated rule w = 40 should appear. Selecting the best of 4–6 configurations on the same runs on which it is compared to Greedy biases the paired differences optimistically; the full grid appears nowhere. *Fix:* apply the rule or state the one used; full-grid table in the supplement; one sentence on selection on the test set.

**C3. α* = 0.45 is post hoc and lies outside the paper's own band criterion. [verified: l.591, l.668, Table C.13]** Criteria A and B give 0.30, C gives nothing; 0.45 is the first grid level that the proposed policy meets on more than half of the pairs, i.e. the tolerance is set to what the method achieves, above the 0.40 "high inequality" bound of criterion B, while Greedy meets 0.35 on 90 % of the pairs. A referee will ask why a platform should adopt a policy less equitable than its current rule for 1.2 coverage points and 1–2 minutes of lateness. *Fix:* present 0.45 as "the tightest target the order-centric policies currently meet" and make the recommendation conditional on valuing the service gain; otherwise the courier-centric rule with α = 0.35.

- l.681 "9 % of its coverage against 5.5 % for the policies that do": the re-optimising policies lose 5.5–7.3 %.

**C5. Sensitivity tables mix three experiments with different bases; the dashes are undefined. [verified: Tables 6 and C.12, l.488 pilot Gini 0.409]** The circuity column rests on one seed, six instances and Greedy only; "--" means three different things (parameter not applicable; configuration not run in X2; statistic not recorded); the pilot Gini of 0.409 (l.488, on which the α grid is "centred") matches neither the eligible statistic (0.301) nor F_G^act (0.509) and the population is not stated; the θ columns' 0.0 base is the five-seed subset while the regime column's is the ten-seed mean. *Fix:* footnote each dash; state the base of every column; move circuity to the text or run κ = 1.0 for the five X2 configurations; say which statistic 0.409 is.

**C6. Announced statistics never reported. [verified: "Wilcoxon" appears once, l.495; eqn:objective and eqn:feasibility are never referenced]** Paired Wilcoxon test (l.495); paired Δ and CI of the second generation versus parent (l.552 claims "excludes zero in every case" but Table 4 has only SEs); F_M, F_O, click-to-door mean and 90th percentile, late orders, solve time per round (§3.2, §5.4); "55 % and 73 % of the days meet α = 0.40" for k_d = 50 (no compliance column in Table C.11); the by-city second-generation values at l.879. *Fix:* add Δ [95 % CI] and compliance columns to Table 4, an appendix table with the unreported metrics and the per-round solve time by policy and city, drop or report the Wilcoxon test; give the number of bootstrap resamples and the interval method.

**C7. Bundling and the emissions mechanism contradict each other. [verified: l.626 vs l.670]** l.626 "Bundling plays no role" (bundle size 1.03 Greedy, 1.00–1.01 others); l.670 attributes Greedy's 25 % extra distance to orders being "less often bundled" and ILP's cleanliness to "the distance saved by bundling". Greedy bundles more than the ILP, so the mechanism is courier choice (least-served first versus nearest). Add a small table with km per order, kg per order and vehicle shares by policy; the numbers now live only in the text.

**C8. The zone construction oversupplies couriers by a factor of about three, and the 21 % undeliverable orders are a boundary effect. [verified: l.225 survey median 2,622 COP/h vs l.626 simulated mean 801 COP/h under Greedy; Table C.6 instance 0: 10,215 orders, 2,650 couriers]** In the field a courier earns about 1.2 orders per hour at 2,200 COP; in the zones about 0.35. Couriers connecting inside the 3-km circle serve the whole city in reality while orders are restricted to drop-offs inside it. The restricted route set (one bundle per store, l.340) is why bundling is nearly switched off while the cited MDRP literature treats it as a main lever. *Fix:* report orders per connected courier-hour in the zone versus the full city, state both artefacts where they first bite (§4.1, §5.1) and in the limitations, and add a sensitivity with the fleet thinned to the field ratio.

**C9. The survey Gini anchors are on a different statistic and population. [verified: l.225, l.668]** Daily income among respondents (active, multi-day bonus regime) versus hourly earnings over eligible couriers with idle ones at zero; the paper's own Table C.10 shows the population moves the statistic by 0.2. State the mismatch; recompute a comparable statistic from the survey if possible, or keep Criterion A and compliance only.

**C10. Experimental bookkeeping. [verified]** Compliance is defined over (instance, seed) pairs but the text says "days" throughout; "window" is used for the two-minute interval, the decision and a two-hour test slice; X0–X3 codes leak into §6–§7 and captions; Table 4 pairs k_d = 8 with w = 20 and k_d = 25 with w = 40 without the w = 20 parent, so w and k_d are confounded; the gen2 caption says 1,920 runs but tabulates five of the eight configurations; MIP-Fair k = 25 ablation "four of the five seeds" (l.771) versus n = 115 (five pairs missing); X2 "1,200 runs" versus three timeouts: say 1,197 and how the three missing pairs enter the paired comparison; "CPU time (min/run)" is wall-clock under 12 parallel processes with turbo disabled, which matters for the 1.3×/5× claims; SE and bootstrap treat 24 instances as exchangeable although they are 3–5 days from six cities (consider a city-blocked bootstrap); seed values, bootstrap resample count and the simulator commit hash are not given.
